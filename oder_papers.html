OderTogether Publication Portfolio

Five peer-reviewed papers examining ecosystem service recovery, angler behavior, and transboundary values following the 2022 Oder River disaster

Portfolio Overview

This research program uses the 2022 Oder River disaster as a natural experiment to examine social-ecological recovery through multiple lenses: revealed preferences (catch logs), stated preferences (ES surveys), behavioral economics (harvest decisions), and cross-cultural values (German-Polish comparison).

  • Papers 1-3: Catch log analysis examining recovery trajectories, satisfaction determinants, and harvest behavior
  • Paper 4: Resident ecosystem services survey analyzing public ES priorities post-disaster
  • Paper 5: Integration paper comparing German-Polish ES valuation and governance preferences

Tier 1: Catch Log Analysis (Revealed Preferences)

Paper 1 • OderAngeln Catch Log Study
Post-Disaster Recovery Trajectory: Recreational Fishing Effort and Satisfaction Following the 2022 Oder River Fish Kill
Temporal analysis • Mixed-effects models • N≈500 anglers, 2-3 years
Research Questions
How have fishing effort, catch rates, and angler satisfaction changed from 2023-2025? Does satisfaction recovery track biological recovery, or do they diverge? Do highly specialized anglers show different recovery patterns than casual anglers?
Key Contributions
  • Rare longitudinal data on social recovery following catastrophic environmental event
  • Tests whether angler satisfaction lags, leads, or tracks biological recovery (CPUE)
  • Extends Birdsong et al. 2021/2022 satisfaction work to post-disaster context
  • Demonstrates specialization as buffer against ecological stress
Data Requirements
Critical: Multi-year catch log data (2023, 2024, 2025 minimum). Without temporal coverage, paper must pivot to cross-sectional design examining 2025 patterns only.
Target: North American Journal of Fisheries Management, Fisheries Research

Paper 2 • OderAngeln Catch Log Study
What Matters When Fish Don't: Angler Motivations, Specialization, and Satisfaction Under Ecological Stress
Cross-sectional analysis • Regression & latent profiles • N≈500 anglers, 2000+ trips
Research Questions
How do catch-oriented vs. nature-oriented motivations predict satisfaction when catch rates are depressed? Does specialization moderate the catch-satisfaction relationship? Can we identify distinct "angler profiles" with different satisfaction drivers?
Key Contributions
  • Tests competing hypotheses: specialized anglers as more vs. less catch-dependent
  • Integrates Beardmore, Ditton, and Danish citizen science (Gundelund) findings
  • Develops motivation proxies from behavioral data (method innovation)
  • Identifies which anglers are resilient vs. vulnerable to poor fishing conditions
Management Implications
Enables targeted retention strategies: specialized anglers need ecosystem health messaging; casual anglers need immediate catch improvements. Informs amenity vs. fish stocking investment decisions.
Target: Human Dimensions of Wildlife, Leisure Sciences

Paper 3 • OderAngeln Catch Log Study
Adaptive Harvest Behavior: How Anglers Adjust Keep Decisions in Response to Declining Fish Populations
Multilevel analysis • Fish-level data • N≈5,000-20,000 individual fish records
Research Questions
Do anglers voluntarily practice more catch-and-release as populations recover slowly? Which factors predict keep vs. release decisions at individual fish level? Does harvest restraint correlate with conservation-oriented characteristics?
Key Contributions
  • Extends Birdsong dissertation Paper IV on voluntary C&R in consumptive culture
  • Tests whether social-ecological feedback loops emerge organically vs. require regulation
  • Three-level analysis (fish within trips within anglers) = methodological sophistication
  • Direct policy relevance: Is voluntary conservation sufficient to aid recovery?
Theoretical Framing
Commons dilemma + norm emergence + specialization theory. Tests whether anglers cooperate to conserve shared resource without enforcement, or whether tragedy of commons prevails.
Target: Fisheries Management & Ecology, Canadian Journal of Fisheries & Aquatic Sciences

Tier 2: Ecosystem Services Analysis (Stated Preferences)

Paper 4 • Resident Ecosystem Services Survey
Whose River, Which Values? Transboundary Ecosystem Service Priorities Following the 2022 Oder River Disaster
Cross-country survey • Factor analysis & regression • N=1,200 residents
Research Questions
How do residents prioritize 14 ecosystem services three years post-disaster? Do German and Polish residents differ in ES priorities despite sharing the same river? What personal characteristics and disaster awareness predict ES valuation?
Key Contributions
  • Applies Kochalski et al. 2022 methodology (IGB multi-country ES framework) to post-disaster context
  • Multiple elicitation methods: importance ratings, Top 3 priorities, budget allocation (100 points)
  • Tests "co-production" framework: ES perception shaped by ecology × society × experience
  • Examines whether environmental crises heighten or diminish ES recognition
Expected Factor Structure
Anticipate 3-4 factors: (1) intrinsic-ecological (biodiversity, fish, future generations), (2) extractive-provisioning (fishing, economic), (3) recreation-cultural (aesthetics, well-being, education), (4) regulating (flood, climate).
Target: Ecosystem Services, Environmental Management, Ecological Economics

Tier 3: Integration & Synthesis

Paper 5 • Integration Across All Data Sources
Transboundary Ecosystem Services: How Shared Rivers Create Unshared Values in the Post-Disaster Oder Basin
Cross-border comparison • Decomposition analysis • All Papers 1-4 data
Core Question
Germans and Poles share the same river and experienced the same disaster—why do they value its ecosystem services differently? Tests three explanations: ecological (different fishing access), economic (development gap), and cultural-institutional (governance traditions).
Key Contributions
  • Natural experiment: same river, same disaster, different national contexts
  • Blinder-Oaxaca decomposition: partition demographics vs. "true" cultural differences
  • Tests whether disaster awareness amplifies or reduces cross-border value divergence
  • Direct policy relevance for EU Water Framework Directive transboundary coordination
  • Three-layer framework: ecology + economy + culture co-produce ES priorities
Hypothesized Patterns
Poles rate extractive ES (fishing, economic) 30-50% higher; Germans rate intrinsic ES (biodiversity, future generations) 10-20% higher; both converge on core ES (water quality, flood protection). Cross-border differences larger in budget allocation than importance ratings—forced tradeoffs reveal priorities.
Bonus Analysis (Data-Dependent)
IF Polish catch log data become available: Compare stated ES priorities to revealed fishing behavior. Do cross-border value differences predict behavioral differences? This would fully integrate Papers 1-5.
Target: Ecosystem Services, Environmental Science & Policy, Global Environmental Change

How Papers Connect

Papers 1-3 (Catch Logs): Provide revealed preference data on how anglers actually behave post-disaster—effort, satisfaction, harvest decisions. Show what anglers DO.

Paper 4 (ES Survey): Provides stated preference data on what residents (including anglers) VALUE. Shows what people SAY is important.

Paper 5 (Integration): Compares stated and revealed preferences across German-Polish border. Tests whether value differences explain behavioral differences. Examines how cultural contexts shape both what people say and what they do.

Continuity with Dissertation: Extends Birdsong et al. 2021 (meta-analysis: catch matters but moderated by characteristics), Birdsong et al. 2022 (specialization moderates satisfaction in German dual-fishery), and Paper IV (voluntary C&R in consumptive culture) to post-disaster social-ecological recovery context.
