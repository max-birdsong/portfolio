First 100 Days Action Plan

Strategic priorities for launching the OderTogether postdoctoral research position

🎤 INTERVIEW KEY - Priority Readings

First 2-3 weeks should include reading these CRITICAL chapters from Pope et al. (2026):

  • Landon et al. (2026) - Social Psychology: Integrated framework, centrality vs. specialization, competing hypotheses (Koemle vs. Beardmore). Foundation for Papers 1-3.
  • Kyle et al. (2026) - Measurement Instruments: Validated scales compendium (DOI: 10.18738/T8/LFWX64), psychometric standards (KMO, α, loadings), pilot testing protocols. Foundation for ALL surveys.
  • de Kerckhove et al. (2026) - Creel Optimization: Sample size formulas (30-40 days), avidity bias correction (70% sampled once!), temporal extrapolation. Foundation for creel design.
  • Kochalski et al. (2026) - Content Analysis: Theoretical saturation, inter-coder reliability, reflexivity. Foundation for open-ended response analysis.
  • Arlinghaus et al. (2026) - Knowledge Co-production: Four-step framework, blue fear, power imbalances, facilitation techniques. Foundation for stakeholder workshops.
  • Vaske et al. (2026) - Survey Guidelines: Question construction, scale length, translation (TRAPD), bias reduction. Foundation for survey development.

These readings inform EVERY methodological decision. Budget 2-3 full days in Weeks 2-3 for deep reading and note-taking.

Guiding Principle: The first 100 days focus on building relationships, understanding existing work, and establishing momentum for data collection and analysis. Success requires balancing immediate analytical work (catch log papers) with longer-term survey development (resident ES, creel module) and stakeholder engagement preparation.

1

Foundation & Orientation

Days 1-30 • Weeks 1-4

Key Objectives

  • Understand OderAngeln data: Access catch log database, review data structure, assess quality and completeness
  • Meet the team: Connect with IFB colleagues, understand roles and ongoing work, identify collaboration opportunities
  • Map stakeholder landscape: Identify key partners (Polish institutions, angling clubs, NGOs, government agencies)
  • Clarify project scope: Confirm timelines, budget, deliverables, and decision authority with PI

Week 1: Administrative & Team Integration

  • Complete onboarding (IT access, email, data systems)
  • Schedule 1-on-1 meetings with each team member
  • Review existing OderTogether documents (grant proposal, work packages, reports)
  • Request access to OderAngeln catch log database
  • Identify who manages Polish partnerships

Week 2-3: Data Assessment & Literature 🎤

  • PRIORITY: Read Pope et al. (2026) chapters (2-3 full days): Landon (Social Psych), Kyle (Measurement), de Kerckhove (Creel), Kochalski (Content Analysis), Arlinghaus (Co-production), Vaske (Survey Guidelines). Take detailed notes—these inform every methodological decision.
  • Explore catch log data: years available, sample sizes, variable completeness
  • Document data quality issues (missing values, outliers, inconsistencies)
  • Review relevant IFB publications and internal reports on the Oder disaster
  • Review Kochalski et al. (2022) salmon ES study—direct methodological template for Paper 4
  • Review your own Birdsong et al. (2021) meta-analysis—cited throughout Pope et al. as THE satisfaction reference
  • Identify whether pre-2022 baseline data exist anywhere (clubs, agencies)
  • Confirm German fishing regulations (size limits, bag limits, closed seasons)

Week 4: Strategic Planning

  • Draft detailed work plan for Year 1 based on data availability
  • Identify quick wins (papers that can start immediately) vs. longer-term projects
  • Schedule regular check-ins with PI (weekly or biweekly)
  • Begin reaching out to German angling clubs for stakeholder mapping

Month 1 Deliverables

  • ✓ Data assessment memo: OderAngeln data structure, quality, limitations, opportunities
  • ✓ Stakeholder map: Key organizations, contacts, relationships
  • ✓ Year 1 work plan: Timeline, milestones, resource needs
  • ✓ Initial catch log descriptives: Sample sizes, temporal coverage, species composition
2

Analysis Launch & Survey Design

Days 31-60 • Weeks 5-8

Key Objectives

  • Begin Paper 1 analysis: Recovery trajectory modeling with existing catch log data
  • Refine survey instruments: Adapt resident ES survey and creel module for German/Polish context
  • Establish stakeholder contacts: Initial outreach to clubs, agencies, Polish partners
  • Plan data collection: Timeline and logistics for resident survey and creel deployment

Week 5-6: Paper 1 - Recovery Trajectory

  • Clean catch log data (remove outliers, create derived variables)
  • Calculate specialization index from background questions
  • Generate descriptive temporal trends (CPUE, satisfaction, effort by year)
  • Run preliminary mixed-effects models (CPUE recovery, satisfaction)
  • Visualize recovery patterns by specialization level
  • Draft methods section and results figures

Week 7: Survey Instrument Refinement 🎤

  • Apply Pope et al. (2026) standards: Use Kyle compendium for validated scales, 5-point Likert (Revilla et al. 2014), construct-specific measures, both sides of scales stated
  • Review resident ES survey with German ecosystem services experts
  • Adapt questions for Oder-specific context (2022 disaster awareness, recovery perceptions)
  • Ensure specialization items measure centrality (psychological commitment) specifically per Landon et al. (2026)
  • Finalize creel survey question order and mobile optimization
  • Begin TRAPD translation (Harkness et al. 2010): Hire professional German→Polish translator, schedule back-translation verification
  • Identify survey panel provider options (USUMA, YouGov, etc.) with German-Polish capacity
  • Budget for cognitive interviews (n=10 per language) in pilot phase

Week 8: Stakeholder Engagement Planning

  • Draft stakeholder engagement strategy document
  • Contact 3-5 German angling clubs near Oder for introductory meetings
  • Identify Polish research partners through existing IFB networks
  • Plan potential workshop formats (online vs. in-person, structure, timing)
  • Reach out to Brandenburg state fisheries agency for coordination

Month 2 Deliverables

  • ✓ Paper 1 preliminary results: Descriptive trends and initial models
  • ✓ Finalized survey instruments: Resident ES and creel module in German
  • ✓ Stakeholder engagement strategy: Workshop plans, outreach timeline
  • ✓ Survey deployment plan: Timeline, sample sizes, budget estimates

🎯 Key Milestone: Paper 1 Draft Introduction + Methods Complete

By Day 60, have a solid draft of Paper 1's intro and methods sections. Results will follow as models are finalized, but establishing the analytical framework early keeps momentum.

Production & Partnerships

Days 61-100 • Weeks 9-14

Key Objectives

  • Complete Paper 1 draft: Full manuscript ready for PI review
  • Launch Paper 2 analysis: Motivation and specialization moderators
  • Survey pilot testing: Cognitive interviews and soft launch preparation
  • Polish partnership development: Identify collaborators for cross-border work

Week 9-10: Paper 1 Completion

  • Create publication-quality figures and tables
  • Write results section with clear hypothesis testing
  • Draft discussion emphasizing management implications
  • Internal review with PI and team members

Week 11-12: Paper 2 Launch & Survey Preparation

  • Run regression models testing specialization and motivation moderation
  • Begin latent profile analysis to identify angler types
  • Coordinate with survey panel provider on sample recruitment
  • Organize cognitive interviews (n=10-15) for resident ES survey

Week 13-14: Partnership Building & Conference Planning

  • Travel to Poland for face-to-face meetings with potential collaborators
  • Present preliminary Paper 1 findings to IFB research group
  • Submit Paper 1 to target journal (North American Journal of Fisheries Management)
  • Draft conference abstract for upcoming fisheries meetings (IIFET, AFS)
  • Plan resident survey pilot launch (target: Month 4)
  • Identify creel survey field staff or contractors

Month 3+ Deliverables (Days 61-100)

  • ✓ Paper 1 submitted to journal
  • ✓ Paper 2 preliminary results: Moderation analyses complete
  • ✓ Cognitive interview report: Survey refinements identified
  • ✓ Polish partnership agreements: MOUs or collaboration letters
  • ✓ Conference abstract submitted
  • ✓ Resident survey pilot ready for soft launch

🎯 Key Milestone: First Paper Submitted + Polish Collaboration Established

By Day 100, demonstrate tangible progress: a journal submission and established cross-border partnerships. This shows both analytical productivity and stakeholder engagement capacity.

100-Day Success Criteria

Publications & Analysis:

  • Paper 1 submitted to peer-reviewed journal
  • Paper 2 analysis 50%+ complete
  • At least one presentation to IFB research group or external audience

Survey Development:

  • Resident ES survey finalized, translated, and cognitively tested
  • Creel module finalized and field-ready
  • Survey deployment timeline established with clear milestones

Stakeholder Engagement:

  • Stakeholder map created with 20+ key contacts identified
  • Established relationships with 3-5 German angling clubs
  • Polish research partnership agreements in place
  • Workshop planning underway with preliminary agenda

Team Integration:

  • Strong working relationships with PI and team members
  • Regular communication rhythm established (weekly check-ins)
  • Clear understanding of project scope, resources, and constraints
  • Identified opportunities for collaboration within IFB

Beyond Day 100: Setting Up Success for Months 4-6

The foundation established in the first 100 days enables accelerated progress in subsequent months:

  • Month 4: Resident survey pilot launch (n=50 per country), Paper 2 draft completion, Paper 3 analysis begins
  • Month 5: Full resident survey deployment (n=1200+), creel survey training and deployment, Paper 1 revisions from peer review
  • Month 6: First stakeholder workshop (preliminary results presentation), Paper 2 submission, integration analysis planning

Flexibility & Contingencies

If catch log data are limited (only 2025):

  • Shift Paper 1 to cross-sectional "baseline assessment" framing
  • Prioritize Paper 2 (doesn't require temporal data) for first submission
  • Defer Paper 3 to Year 2 once 2026 data enable temporal analysis

If Polish partnerships are slow to develop:

  • Launch German-side surveys first (de-risk timeline)
  • Treat Polish component as Phase 2 (months 6-12)
  • Focus stakeholder engagement on German clubs initially

If survey budget is constrained:

  • Reduce sample size to minimum viable (n=400 per country for resident survey)
  • Prioritize creel over resident survey (more directly tied to catch log data)
  • Explore student helpers for creel intercepts (reduce contractor costs)
