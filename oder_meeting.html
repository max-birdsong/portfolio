Sample Stakeholder Workshop

Transboundary Oder River Ecosystem Services: Preliminary Research Findings & Stakeholder Perspectives

🎤 INTERVIEW KEY - Co-Production Framework

Based on Arlinghaus et al. (2026) "Knowledge Co-production for Recreational Fisheries" in Pope et al. (2026)

  • Four-Step Framework (Bath 2010): (1) Build relationships with diverse actors (listening phase, one-on-one interviews, common ground matrix), (2) Understand consensus and disagreement (surveys, secondary data), (3) Implement facilitated workshop series (repeated interactions), (4) Evaluate process (beyond goal achievement)
  • Trust takes TIME: "Each co-production relationship fundamentally requires trust and relationship building, which necessitates large investments in face-to-face time... Successful co-production initiatives can easily take years to occur" (p.541). 2-year postdoc = co-ASSESSMENT, not full co-production.
  • "Blue Fear": "Fear that outcomes of research may have negative consequences such as motivating stricter regulatory control" (p.541). German anglers may fear data will justify more restrictions post-disaster. Address through transparency about data use and decision-making.
  • Power Imbalances: Scientists with formal training vs. stakeholders without creates advantage in interpreting data. Solution: neutral facilitator, transparent data sharing, co-interpret findings together.
  • Professional Facilitation REQUIRED: "Formal workshops often need to be professionally moderated" (p.543). Poor facilitation "can quickly degrade a process." Budget for external facilitator with transboundary experience.
  • German Anglers = Rights Holders: "Angler communities in central Europe who are rights holders in local ﬁsheries" (p.544). Higher ethical bar—OCAP principles apply (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession).
  • Besatzfisch Example: IGB's 5-year German stocking co-production (2009-2014) changed attitudes AND practices. Won 2014 UNESCO Education Award. Demonstrates feasibility—but requires long-term commitment.

Workshop 1: Preliminary Findings & Stakeholder Input

Building shared understanding of Oder River recovery dynamics and angler perspectives

Workshop Objectives

  • Share preliminary findings from catch log analysis (Papers 1-3) on angler recovery patterns, satisfaction, and harvest behavior

Detailed Agenda

9:00 - 9:30
Welcome & Orientation
30 min
Purpose
Set tone, establish ground rules, and create inclusive atmosphere for transboundary dialogue
Activities
  • Welcome (5 min): PI opens workshop, thanks participants, introduces research team
  • Participant introductions (15 min): Round-robin: Name, organization, one hope for today's discussion (use timer: 30 seconds each)
  • Workshop overview (5 min): Present agenda, objectives, ground rules
  • Logistics (5 min): Translation protocol, break schedule, where to find materials
Ground Rules
  • Respectful dialogue across perspectives
  • One person speaks at a time
  • Questions welcomed; note "parking lot" for off-agenda items
  • Chatham House Rule: Ideas can be shared, but not attributed without permission

Facilitator Notes:

Build rapport early. Use icebreaker to help German/Polish participants connect. Emphasize that all perspectives are valuable—researchers don't have all answers. This is collaborative learning.

9:30 - 10:15
Context: The 2022 Disaster & Recovery Efforts
45 min
Purpose
Establish shared factual baseline about the disaster, ecological impacts, and response efforts. Ensure all participants operating from common understanding.
Presentations
  • Disaster timeline & impacts (10 min): IFB ecologist presents biological data on fish mortality, species affected, spatial extent
  • Response & recovery actions (10 min): Government agency rep summarizes restocking, habitat work, monitoring programs
  • Stakeholder perspectives (15 min): 2-3 pre-selected speakers (1 German angler, 1 Polish community member, 1 NGO) share 5-minute personal accounts of how disaster affected them
Q&A
10 minutes for clarifying questions only (save discussion for later sessions)

Facilitator Notes:

Keep this factual and non-controversial. Goal is alignment, not debate. Pre-coordinate with speakers to avoid surprises. Have slides in both German and Polish. Acknowledge emotional weight of topic—this was traumatic for many.

10:15 - 10:30
Coffee Break
15 min
10:30 - 11:45
Research Findings: Angler Recovery Patterns (Paper 1)
75 min
Purpose
Present catch log findings on fishing effort, catch rates, and satisfaction trends 2023-2025. Validate findings with stakeholder knowledge and identify surprises.
Presentation
15 minutes: Researcher presents key findings:
  • CPUE trends: Did catch rates improve? By how much? Seasonal patterns?
  • Satisfaction trajectory: Are anglers happier over time? Does it track with CPUE?
  • Effort patterns: Who kept fishing? Who stopped? Differences by specialization?
  • Management implications: What does recovery look like from angler perspective?
Structured Discussion
60 minutes, facilitated discussion using these prompts:
  • Reality check (20 min): "Do these patterns match what you've observed? Where do findings align with your experience? Where do they diverge?"
  • Explanation seeking (20 min): "What might explain the trends we're seeing? Local knowledge that helps interpret data?"
  • Missing pieces (20 min): "What important aspects of recovery aren't captured by catch rates and satisfaction? What are we missing?"

Facilitator Notes:

Facilitation approach: Use "round-robin" for first question (everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice). Use raised hands for second/third questions. Actively invite quieter voices. If discussion goes off-track, acknowledge comment, note in "parking lot," redirect to prompt. Watch for German-Polish balance—ensure both nationalities heard equally.

11:45 - 12:00
Synthesis: What We Heard
15 min
Purpose
Capture key themes from discussion; ensure participants feel heard
Process
  • Facilitator summarizes 3-5 main themes from discussion (prepared during discussion using notes)
  • Ask: "Did I capture that accurately? What did I miss?"
  • Note: "These perspectives will inform our interpretation of results and guide survey design"
12:00 - 13:00
Lunch
60 min
Format
Buffet lunch provided. Encourage cross-border conversation. Research team available for informal discussion.
13:00 - 14:00
Research Findings: Motivation, Specialization & Harvest (Papers 2-3)
60 min
Purpose
Present findings on what sustains angler satisfaction and whether harvest behavior changed post-disaster
Presentations
Paper 2 (10 min): Motivation & specialization findings
  • Which anglers maintained satisfaction despite poor catch rates?
  • What besides catching fish matters for satisfaction?
  • Angler typology: Different groups with different needs
Paper 3 (10 min): Harvest behavior findings
  • Did harvest rates change 2023-2025?
  • Who practices catch-and-release? Why?
  • Evidence of voluntary conservation or not?
Discussion
40 minutes: Focus on management relevance
  • Do these typologies make sense? Would different messaging strategies work?
  • Is voluntary conservation sufficient or are stricter regulations needed?
  • How can managers support anglers during recovery?

Facilitator Notes:

Harvest behavior can be sensitive (some anglers defensive about keeping fish). Frame as curiosity, not judgment. Emphasize goal is understanding what's happening, not prescribing what "should" happen. If debate gets heated between conservationists and consumptive anglers, acknowledge both perspectives are valid and ask: "What information would help resolve this tension?"

14:00 - 15:15
Breakout Groups: Resident Survey Priorities
75 min
Purpose
Gather input on upcoming resident ecosystem services survey. Ensure it addresses stakeholder priorities and captures what matters to communities.
Setup
Introduction (10 min): Explain survey scope, show draft question areas, describe breakout task
Breakout Groups
45 minutes in 3 mixed groups (8-10 people each):
  • Each group assigned 2 ecosystem service categories (e.g., Group 1: Provisioning + Cultural)
  • Task: "What questions should we ask residents? What's most important to measure?"
  • Groups write ideas on flip charts
  • Ensure mix of German/Polish, anglers/non-anglers, government/NGO/community in each group
Report Back
20 minutes: Each group presents top 3-5 priorities (5 min each), facilitator synthesizes common themes

Facilitator Notes:

Assign strong facilitator to each breakout (pre-briefed on task). Provide clear instructions and time warnings. Groups should feel ownership over survey design—this increases buy-in for results later. Capture all ideas (photos of flip charts) for analysis after workshop.

Facilitation Strategy

Drawing on experience facilitating Gulf of Mexico Fishery Management Council meetings AND Arlinghaus et al. (2026) knowledge co-production framework from IGB's successful Besatzfisch project, this workshop emphasizes clear scope definition, structured participation, and inclusive dialogue across diverse stakeholder groups.

Key Facilitation Principles 🎤

Based on Arlinghaus et al. (2026) co-production guidelines: Professional facilitation is essential. Budget for external facilitator with transboundary experience. Poor facilitation "can quickly degrade a process."

1. Clear Scope & Purpose (Arlinghaus: "Define What Co-Production Means")

Every session has explicit objectives and defined scope. Participants know what's in-bounds vs. out-of-bounds. Use "parking lot" for important but off-agenda items. Critical: Clarify that this is co-ASSESSMENT (presenting findings, jointly interpreting), not full co-production (which takes 5+ years). Set realistic expectations.

2. Structured Participation (Arlinghaus: "Facilitation Techniques")

Use specific techniques from Arlinghaus et al. (2026, p.546-547):

  • Silent discussion: Participants write ideas on sticky notes, facilitator groups by theme. Reduces dominance by vocal participants.
  • Five-times-why: Repeatedly ask "why?" to get to root causes of disagreements, not just surface positions.
  • Small group rotation: Break into mixed German-Polish groups, rotate, cross-pollinate ideas.
  • Nominal group technique: Structured turn-taking ensures equal voice.

3. Cultural & Language Sensitivity + Power Balance

Monitor German-Polish balance. Pause for translation. Acknowledge different cultural norms around conflict and directness. Frame disagreements as productive diversity, not problems. Address power imbalances (Arlinghaus et al. p.543): Scientists have advantage interpreting data—mitigate by presenting findings in accessible formats, co-interpreting results, and valuing local knowledge equally.

4. Active Listening & Synthesis

Paraphrase complex comments to ensure understanding. Identify common ground across perspectives. Synthesize themes periodically. Show how input shapes next steps. Document EVERYTHING: Transparent record of how stakeholder input influences decisions builds trust.

5. Conflict Management + "Blue Fear" Mitigation

Anticipate tension points (harvest regulations, conservation priorities). Acknowledge competing interests. Redirect to shared goals (healthy river, vibrant fishery). Focus on interests, not positions. Address "blue fear" explicitly (Arlinghaus et al. p.541): German anglers may fear data will justify stricter regulations. Be transparent: "This research informs management but doesn't dictate policy. Your participation helps ensure decisions reflect stakeholder priorities."

6. Time Management + Social Connection

Assign timekeeper. Give 5-minute warnings before transitions. Be willing to cut discussion if unproductive, extend if valuable. Respect lunch and break times. Arlinghaus emphasis (p.543): "Developing relationships is ultimately about sharing time and own values, meeting face-to-face, having meals together and thereby building trust." BUDGET for quality catering, social breaks, informal conversation time.

Success Metrics

This workshop will be considered successful if:

  • ≥80% of invited stakeholders attend
  • Both German and Polish perspectives represented roughly equally in discussions
  • All participant groups (anglers, government, NGOs, community, research) contribute meaningfully
  • Concrete survey design input gathered that can be implemented
  • Post-workshop feedback shows ≥75% found it "valuable" or "very valuable"
  • Stakeholders express interest in attending Workshop 2
  • No major conflicts or walkouts (disagreement is fine; breakdown is not)
